Q: What areas of AI have you worked on?

A: I have worked on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), Large Language Model integration, Prompt Engineering, Explainable AI (XAI), Machine Learning, Vector Databases, Embedding Models, and Human-in-the-Loop AI systems.

---

Q: What is Generative AI?

A: Generative AI refers to AI systems capable of creating new content such as text, images, code, audio, or videos based on patterns learned from training data.

---

Q: What is an LLM?

A: A Large Language Model (LLM) is an AI model trained on massive amounts of text data that can understand and generate human-like language.

---

Q: Which LLMs have you worked with?

A: Through my Personal AI Chatbot and Cognizant GenAI training, I have worked with LLaMA-based models through the Groq API.

---

Q: What is RAG?

A: Retrieval-Augmented Generation combines information retrieval with LLM generation. Relevant information is first retrieved from a knowledge base and then supplied to the LLM before generating an answer.

---

Q: Why is RAG better than using an LLM alone?

A: LLMs can hallucinate or provide outdated information. RAG grounds responses using retrieved information from a trusted knowledge base, improving accuracy and reliability.

---

Q: Why did you choose RAG for your chatbot?

A: Recruiters need accurate information about my profile. RAG allows the chatbot to answer using my knowledge base rather than relying solely on model knowledge.

---

Q: Explain the architecture of your chatbot.

A: Knowledge Base → Chunking → Embedding Generation → ChromaDB Storage → Semantic Retrieval → Groq LLM → Final Response.

---

Q: What are embeddings?

A: Embeddings are numerical vector representations of text that capture semantic meaning.

---

Q: Why are embeddings needed?

A: They allow semantic similarity search. Instead of matching exact keywords, the system can retrieve information based on meaning.

---

Q: Which embedding model do you use?

A: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 from Sentence Transformers.

---

Q: Why choose all-MiniLM-L6-v2?

A: It provides a good balance between speed, memory efficiency, and retrieval quality.

---

Q: What is a vector database?

A: A vector database stores embeddings and enables efficient similarity search.

---

Q: Which vector database did you use?

A: ChromaDB.

---

Q: Why ChromaDB?

A: ChromaDB is lightweight, open source, easy to integrate, and well suited for personal RAG applications.

---

Q: What is semantic search?

A: Semantic search retrieves information based on meaning rather than exact keyword matching.

---

Q: What chunking strategy do you use?

A: Chunk size of 300 characters with an overlap of 50 characters.

---

Q: Why use overlap?

A: Overlap helps preserve context across chunk boundaries and improves retrieval quality.

---

Q: What is cosine similarity?

A: Cosine similarity measures how similar two vectors are by comparing the angle between them.

---

Q: How does retrieval work in your chatbot?

A: User query is embedded, compared against stored embeddings, and the most similar chunks are retrieved before being passed to the LLM.

---

Q: What is confidence gating?

A: Confidence gating prevents the chatbot from answering when retrieval confidence is too low.

---

Q: Why did you implement confidence gating?

A: To reduce hallucinations and improve trustworthiness.

---

Q: What confidence threshold do you use?

A: 0.45.

---

Q: What happens below the threshold?

A: The chatbot refuses to answer and may request additional information instead of generating an unreliable response.

---

Q: What is prompt engineering?

A: Prompt engineering is the process of designing prompts that guide LLMs toward desired outputs.

---

Q: How did you use prompt engineering?

A: In the chatbot and JD matcher, I carefully structured prompts to improve retrieval grounding, answer quality, and requirement extraction.

---

Q: What is a hallucination?

A: A hallucination occurs when an LLM generates incorrect or fabricated information.

---

Q: How does RAG reduce hallucinations?

A: By supplying retrieved factual context before answer generation.

---

Q: How does confidence gating reduce hallucinations?

A: It prevents response generation when the system lacks sufficient supporting evidence.

---

Q: What is Human-in-the-Loop AI?

A: Human-in-the-Loop systems allow humans to review, correct, and improve AI outputs.

---

Q: How did you implement Human-in-the-Loop learning?

A: Unanswered recruiter questions are stored. I later answer them manually and re-ingest them into the knowledge base.

---

Q: What are the benefits of Human-in-the-Loop systems?

A: Continuous improvement, higher accuracy, better reliability, and reduced knowledge gaps.

---

Q: What is a knowledge base?

A: A structured collection of information used by the retrieval system to answer questions.

---

Q: Why is knowledge base quality important?

A: Retrieval quality directly depends on the quality, completeness, and organization of stored information.

---

Q: What is retrieval quality?

A: Retrieval quality measures how effectively relevant information is identified and returned.

---

Q: What is vector similarity search?

A: It finds embeddings that are closest to a query embedding using similarity metrics.

---

Q: What is context injection?

A: Context injection refers to supplying retrieved information to the LLM before generation.

---

Q: Why not put all information directly into prompts?

A: Large knowledge bases exceed context windows and increase cost and latency.

---

Q: What is an AI agent?

A: An AI agent is a system that can reason, make decisions, use tools, and execute actions toward a goal.

---

Q: What is Agentic AI?

A: Agentic AI refers to AI systems capable of planning, tool usage, reasoning, and multi-step decision making.

---

Q: What exposure do you have to Agentic AI?

A: I completed GenAI and Agentic AI training during my Cognizant internship.

---

Q: What is Explainable AI (XAI)?

A: Explainable AI helps users understand why a model produced a specific prediction.

---

Q: Why is Explainable AI important?

A: It increases transparency, trust, accountability, and adoption of AI systems.

---

Q: Which Explainable AI technique did you use?

A: SHAP.

---

Q: What is SHAP?

A: SHAP explains predictions by measuring the contribution of individual features.

---

Q: Where did you use SHAP?

A: In the AI-Based Symptom Diagnosis System.

---

Q: How did SHAP improve your diagnosis system?

A: It allowed users to see which symptoms contributed most to a disease prediction.

---

Q: What machine learning model did you use?

A: Random Forest.

---

Q: Why Random Forest?

A: It achieved better accuracy and integrated well with SHAP explainability.

---

Q: What accuracy did your model achieve?

A: 92.28%.

---

Q: What dataset did you use?

A: A Kaggle healthcare dataset containing over 246,000 patient records.

---

Q: What is feature engineering?

A: Feature engineering is the process of creating or transforming variables to improve model performance.

---

Q: What feature engineering techniques did you use?

A: Symptom normalization, fuzzy matching, synonym mapping, and preprocessing techniques.

---

Q: What is fuzzy matching?

A: Fuzzy matching identifies similar text values even when they are not exact matches.

---

Q: Why use fuzzy matching?

A: Real-world users often describe symptoms differently.

---

Q: What is synonym mapping?

A: Synonym mapping converts multiple equivalent symptom descriptions into a standardized representation.

---

Q: What did AI projects teach you?

A: They taught me problem formulation, data preparation, explainability, retrieval systems, prompt engineering, and how to build trustworthy AI solutions.
